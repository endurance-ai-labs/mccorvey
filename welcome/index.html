How it works

Feed → Engine → Exhibit.

No downloads, no uploads, no rebuilt workbooks. The number a foreman logs is the number the forecast carries and the number the owner sees.

01

Live feeds in

Financials stream from QuickBooks Desktop; tickets, timesheets and agreements from ServiceTitan. Rates and market context arrive from the public data complex — refreshed on schedule, stamped with provenance.

02

One engine

A single calculation layer builds the job forecasts, the monthly workbook, the manpower math and the five-year model. Hover any output and it shows its inputs — the arithmetic is never a black box.

03

Institutional exhibits out

Board-ready on screen, client-ready on export: G702 applications, payment packages with waivers, submittal packages, proposals with e-signatures, styled Excel and letterhead print.

Controls

Built for sign-off, not just display.

Institutional control is a workflow, not a watermark. Every financial exhibit carries a maker-checker chain — and the chain chases itself.

Sequential approvals

Office → Controller → CFO on the forecast cycle and billing close; PM → Owner on change orders and proposals. A stage cannot sign before the one ahead of it.

Named, timestamped stamps

Every sign-off records who, in what role, at what time — visible on the exhibit itself. Role-based access decides who sees margins at all.

E-signatures end to end

Proposals and change orders are e-signed in role by the PM, then sent to the customer for a typed counter-signature — fully executed without a printer.

Slack-connected nudges

Waiting on someone? One click messages them on Slack with the exact step, page and link — and the chain stamps when the reminder went out.
