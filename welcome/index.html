The flagship · AI Bid & Document Review

Three million dollars a year
goes to reading paper.

Every set gets machine-read in minutes — the estimator reviews a finished brief instead of turning 1,420 pages. Nothing goes out without a named estimator sign-off, and the arithmetic behind every extracted quantity is one click away.

01

Every page, ingested

Spec books, drawing sets and every addendum land in the portal the moment they hit the plan room. The AI reads the full set — mechanical specs, gauge schedules, general conditions — before anyone opens a PDF.

02

Quantities & traps, extracted

Pounds of rectangular duct, linear feet of spiral, damper and GRD counts — pulled with page-level citations. Spec traps get flagged in red: gauge-schedule swaps buried in Addendum 3, liquidated damages, leakage-testing scope shifts, closed substitution lists.

03

Estimator sign-off

A senior estimator confirms or corrects the brief in about 3.5 hours instead of 42 — every override recorded, every set stamped. Across ~620 sets a year, that's roughly $2.4M of review cost back on the table.

→

Addenda that can't hide

Each addendum is diffed against the set it amends. When Addendum 3 quietly replaces the gauge schedule from Division 23, the change shows up as a red-line — with the old spec, the new spec, and the tonnage impact side by side.

 →

From brief to bid

The signed-off brief flows straight into the bid builder and the pipeline — extracted quantities become the takeoff baseline, flagged risks become bid qualifications, and the go/no-go call is made on a read set, not a skimmed one.

P1

Plant 1 — Wallisville Rd

Rectangular and welded fabrication. The shop board sequences releases from BIM coordination through cut, weld, insulate and ship — load-levelled against booked install dates and STRATUS spool packages.

Access & the Operating Brain

Built for sign-off, not just display.

Institutional control is a workflow, not a watermark. Every financial exhibit carries a maker-checker chain, every role sees exactly what it should — and an AI assistant rides along on every page.

Role-based access

Estimator, PM, shop foreman, controller, executive — each role card opens its own view. Margins, rates and Duct Direct account terms are visible only to the roles entitled to them.

Sequential approvals

Office → Controller → CFO on the forecast cycle and billing close; estimator sign-off on every AI-reviewed bid set; PM → Owner on change orders. A stage cannot sign before the one ahead of it.

Named, timestamped stamps

Every sign-off records who, in what role, at what time — visible on the exhibit itself, from a G702 application to a bid-review brief.

The Operating Brain

Bottom-right on every page: ask it anything the book of record knows — a job's forecast-at-completion, which addendum changed the gauge schedule, Plant 2's load next month — and it answers from the data, with sources.
